Workout Pledge vs StepBet vs Forfeit

All three apps use real money to keep you consistent — but they solve different problems. Here's a plain, honest comparison so you can pick the one that actually fits your goal.

📲  Get Workout Pledge on iOS

01The Short Version

StepBet is for step goals: you join a ~$40, six-week game, hit personalized daily step targets, and split the pot with other winners if you make it. Forfeit is for any habit: you stake money on a task and submit photo or timelapse evidence when you've done it. Workout Pledge is for one thing — building a real workout habit. You set a weekly goal of 30-minute workout days, pledge a dollar amount per missed day, and your Apple Watch or iPhone verifies the workout automatically. Hit your goal and you earn points, funded by the pledges of people who didn't follow through, redeemable for gift cards or donatable to a nonprofit.

One thing we want to be upfront about: none of this is betting — at least not the way we built it. There's no chance involved and no house to beat. Whether you keep your money is decided entirely by whether you do the thing you said you'd do. That's a commitment contract, and it's the same loss-aversion principle behavioral economists have studied since 1979 (here's the science).

03When StepBet Is the Better Pick

Choose StepBet if walking is your workout and you enjoy a game with a start line, a finish line, and other people in the pot. The six-week format gives you a defined challenge, and personalized step goals meet you where you are. Its limits are the flip side of its design: steps are the only currency, so a hard strength session, a swim, or a yoga class counts for little — and if one bad week knocks you out of a game, your motivation (and your $40) can exit with it.

04When Forfeit Is the Better Pick

Choose Forfeit if you want stakes on everything — waking up early, studying, shipping a project — not just fitness. It's a flexible commitment tool with real teeth. The trade-off is friction: evidence is something you submit, photo or timelapse, task by task. For a daily workout habit specifically, that friction adds up, and self-submitted evidence is inherently softer verification than sensor data recorded by your watch.

05When Workout Pledge Is the Better Pick

Choose Workout Pledge if your goal is the one most people actually have: work out consistently, week after week. Three design choices follow from that goal. First, any workout counts — 30 minutes of lifting, running, cycling, yoga, or a brisk walk, alone or stacked across the day. Second, verification is automatic and hard to fudge: it comes from your device's sensors through Apple Health, not from a check-in or a photo. Third, the stakes renew weekly: there's no game to be eliminated from, just next week's goal — so one bad week costs you a few dollars, not your momentum.

And because the outcome is entirely in your hands, it's a pledge — a promise you make to yourself — not a wager. If you're curious why that small psychological distinction matters so much, read about the science of loss aversion, or see what happened to Pact, the app that pioneered this category a decade ago.
